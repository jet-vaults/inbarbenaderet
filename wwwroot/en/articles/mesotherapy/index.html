Mesotherapy / Microneedling

Microneedling uses micro-needles to prompt the skin to produce fresh collagen and new tissue - smoother, firmer, younger-looking skin. Well suited to scarring, wrinkles, and open pores.

Microneedling is a treatment in which tiny needles are used to create micro-punctures in the skin. The aim is to stimulate the production of collagen and new skin tissue - for smoother, firmer, younger-looking skin. It is well suited to scarring, wrinkles, and open pores, and can also be used to fade acne scars, sun spots, and dark circles under the eyes.

How Is a Microneedling Session Performed?

The purpose of the treatment is to prompt the skin to produce collagen - the protein responsible, among other things, for the skin's elasticity and freshness. The body loses collagen naturally with age and following injury. The idea is simple: the micro-needles create a light, controlled injury, and the skin responds by building new, collagen-rich tissue - firmer, and more even in tone and texture.

During the session I make tiny punctures with a pen-like device, moving it evenly across the treated area. The needles are so fine they are barely felt; there can be some discomfort, but it is entirely tolerable. Before the treatment, we apply active ingredients formulated to penetrate deep into the cells.

Who Is Microneedling For?

Microneedling suits healthy men and women dealing with one or more of the following:

  • Acne scarring
  • Age spots or sun spots
  • Wrinkles and fine lines
  • Open pores
  • Loss of skin elasticity
  • Uneven skin tone

Microneedling is also sometimes used to treat stretch marks on other areas of the body, such as the thighs and abdomen, and to soften the appearance of scars elsewhere.

Session Length and What to Expect Afterwards

A session lasts between 45 minutes and an hour, with no anaesthetics. The skin looks red afterwards, and the redness may intensify - for up to 24 hours at most. The skin then looks wonderful, with a marked improvement in texture, tone, and fine lines.

Treatment is delivered as a series; the number of sessions is set according to the goal you arrive with.

Who Should Avoid It?

The treatment is not intended for pregnant women, for those with chronic skin conditions such as psoriasis or eczema, for anyone with open wounds or a history of keloid scarring, for those who have recently undergone radiotherapy, for oncology patients, or for people with unbalanced diabetes.
